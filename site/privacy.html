Coo

Privacy

Coo is a mail and calendar app that runs on your Mac. This page explains, plainly, what happens to your data.

Your mail and calendars stay on your Mac

Coo connects directly from your Mac to your providers (Microsoft, Google and Apple iCloud) using their official interfaces. It keeps a copy of your recent mail and events on your computer so it can be fast and work offline. Nothing is sent to servers run by Coo; there are none.

Sign-ins

Microsoft and Google sign-ins happen on their own pages; Coo receives a token that lets it read and send mail and calendar events for you. iCloud uses an app-specific password you create at Apple. Tokens and passwords are stored in your Mac's Keychain. Signing out in Settings removes them and the account's local data.

What Coo does with Google and Microsoft data

  • Shows your mail and calendars, searches them, and lets you send, reply, move, archive, and answer invitations.
  • Does not sell, share or transfer your data to anyone, use it for advertising, or use it to train AI models.
  • Only acts when you ask it to (or, if you set it up, when an AI assistant you connect on your own Mac asks it to).

Coo's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Crash reports

If Coo hits an error, it can send an anonymous report (to Sentry) so it can be fixed. Email addresses, message text, subjects, names and web-address details are removed on your Mac before anything is sent. You can turn crash reports off in Settings › Privacy.

Updates

Coo checks GitHub for new versions. That request contains only the app version, like any download.
